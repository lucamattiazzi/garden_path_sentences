Garden-Path Sentences in Gemma 3

Mechanistic-interpretability pipeline (steps 01–14) run on the Gemma 3 base-model family: 1B, 4B, 12B and 27B. All models are pre-trained checkpoints (-pt), evaluated in the same harness used for the Qwen3 investigation.

Overview

A garden-path sentence (GPS), such as “The old man the boat”, has a temporarily ambiguous structure. This exploratory pipeline measures predictive differences and their sensitivity to interventions. Neither metric alone identifies a committed parse, comprehension, or syntactic reanalysis.

  • Δ-surprisal: S(critical | GPS prefix) − S(critical | normal prefix).
  • vc_delta: S(continuation | GPS + critical) − S(continuation | normal + critical). Continuation results cover NV items only, not all five syntactic templates.

These are historical logs, not reruns with the reviewed code. See the reproducible numerical audit for provenance checks and item sensitivity.

01 · Baseline garden-path effect

Mean Δ-surprisal at the designated critical word across 50 GPS/normal pairs: RR, NV, NP_S, NP_Z and COORD. A positive value indicates lower probability in the GPS condition; it does not establish that the model committed to an incorrect parse. The hand-authored materials include early disambiguation and lexical/semantic confounds; they have not been independently validated as uniformly eliciting garden paths.

The per-pair distribution below shows the effect is heterogeneous: some pairs produce large spikes, others none (or even negative deltas, where the “normal” paraphrase happens to be the more awkward string).

02 · Layer sweep — where does the surprise signal live?

All attention heads in each layer are zero-ablated in turn; the MLP remains active. The stored convention is effect_drop = Δ under ablation − baseline Δ: negative values indicate a reduction of the effect. Control inflation measures the change on eight unambiguous probes. Small control inflation is a limited check against general damage, not sufficient evidence of GPS specificity. X-axis: layer / n_layers.

03 · Head sweeps within candidate layers

Individual heads are ablated within layers selected by step 02. Negative effect_drop means the measured Δ decreased. These exploratory candidates require independent validation; weak individual effects do not exclude redundant, distributed or MLP-mediated mechanisms.

04 · Do the surprise heads matter for continuation prediction?

Candidate heads from steps 02–03 are ablated individually and together while measuring vc_delta, the GPS-minus-normal continuation cost. A larger gap indicates a differential predictive effect. Either outcome leaves open the role of syntax, lexical expectations and redundancy. The first bar is the unablated baseline.

05 · Layer sweep on the continuation prediction metric

Per-layer ablation scored by vc_delta inflation: the change in the GPS-minus-normal continuation cost. Dashed control traces are available in the legend. This localises influences on the predictive proxy, not a reanalysis process itself.

06 · Head sweeps on the continuation prediction metric

Individual heads in layers selected by step 05 are ablated while measuring vc_gps inflation. Compare GPS, normal and control effects together. The dashed step-14 threshold is a descriptive max-of-K random-head reference. It accounts for choosing a maximum over K draws but not the preceding data-dependent layer selection. Whole-layer and single-head effects need not be additive; this alone does not identify cooperative computation or exclude a circuit.

07/08 · Lexical floor — canonical vs novel NV pairs

Continuation surprisal after the full GPS prefix, a stripped local prefix, and a neutral anchor. Historical labels “canonical” and “novel” denote the original and additional sets of ten NV items; absence from training data has not been verified for either set. Bootstrap intervals describe full − stripped across items. This diagnostic cannot distinguish memorisation from parsing, regardless of whether the interval spans zero.

09 · Continuation cost with confidence intervals

Mean vc_delta with percentile-bootstrap 95% intervals over ten items per set. Positive values indicate a continuation prediction gap, not necessarily incomplete reanalysis. Item effects are heterogeneous. Scale comparisons require paired differences on the same items; overlapping intervals alone are not a test of a difference.

10 · Attention patterns — does anything look back at the ambiguous word?

The heatmaps show attention from the disambiguator to the ambiguous word, GPS minus normal. The tables rank heads by absolute mean difference, but the saved Spearman correlation uses per-pair GPS attention (not GPS-minus-normal attention) against vc_delta. Correlations are exploratory, uncorrected for multiple comparisons and do not establish a causal retrospective parsing operation.

11 · Activation patching (NORMAL → GPS)

NORMAL residual states are copied into GPS at the ambiguous word, disambiguator, or both. recovery = (mean s_gps − mean s_patched) / (mean s_gps − mean s_normal). This transfers all information in the residual, including semantics and lexical predictions. At the last layer, copying the state before a single-token target reproduces its source prediction by construction. Earlier ambiguous-word states have not seen the disambiguator. Intermediate effects establish influence on the proxy, not syntax-specific reanalysis; ratios above one indicate overshoot and become unstable for small baseline gaps.

12 · Zero vs mean ablation

Selected surprise heads/head sets and continuation-sensitive layers are compared under zero and pooled-mean ablation. Mean replacement also changes the conditional activation distribution; it does not preserve variance or contextual dependencies. Agreement is a robustness observation, while disagreement indicates intervention sensitivity. Neither outcome alone decides whether an effect is an artifact. This step does not automatically replicate the individual continuation heads selected in step 06.

13 · Empirical null distribution

Random single-head ablations provide a descriptive reference for the NV-only effect, whereas the step-03 headline averages all templates. These distributions include real head effects and are not a null hypothesis of no effect. Historical Qwen14B logs contain a counterexample to the earlier “no head exceeded the null” claim: L10.H25 changes NV Δ by −0.134 nats, versus a sampled absolute maximum of 0.088 across 60 random heads. This comparison alone is not selection-corrected evidence of a circuit.

14 · Empirical null for the continuation prediction metric

Random heads are evaluated on the original ten NV items. The dotted threshold is the bootstrap p95 of the maximum over K draws from this empirical reference, where K counts the heads swept in step 06. This is not a confirmatory significance threshold: it omits prior layer selection, reuses the exploration items, and cannot estimate effects beyond the sampled tail. Not exceeding it does not establish absence of a mechanism.
